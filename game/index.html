You are the membrane

Deep in the bulk, two logical qubits hold a pattern. A holographic tensor network — 3 layers of tensors, 6 · 7 · 6 — encodes it outward onto 7 boundary qubits. The pattern survives out there, smeared across the whole boundary… until something attacks it.

Across 15 episodes, a state arrives at your boundary. It might be a real pattern, damaged by erasure or noise. It might be a collision — two patterns superposed — or pure randomness wearing a pattern's shape. Your call, every time:

  • PROJECT — name the pattern and push it through the gate, with a confidence of 50–99%. Calibration scoring: high confidence pays more when you're right — and costs more when you're wrong.
  • ABSTAIN — refuse. Costs a little when the pattern was real; saves you when it was fake or a collision.
  • Sealed decoder — once per episode, open the reference channel for −15 pts to learn the true recovery fidelity before you decide.

The run, in numbers

What the membrane taught: the bulk survives 2 erasures by distinguishability (0.973 → 0.919 → 0.737), and a real decoder stretches that to k = 4 at ε = 0.01, k = 3 at ε = 0.1, k = 1 at ε = 0.5 — recovery is a noise budget, not a promise. No threshold separates a real pattern from a clever fake, and no gate setting detects a collision: a superposition is always accepted and named as one of its pair. Knowing when to abstain is the whole game.
